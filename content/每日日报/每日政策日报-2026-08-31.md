[Hybrid HBM-HBF Architecture in LLM Inference (University of Oxford)](https://semiengineering.com/hybrid-hbm-hbf-architecture-in-llm-inference-university-of-oxford/) 
**Summary:**  
Researchers from the University of Oxford, including Atassi, Zilberman, and Awad, published a paper in *IEEE Computer Architecture Letters* (August 2026) titled **"Hardware-Managed Heterogeneous High-Bandwidth Memory and Flash in LLM Inference Systems."** The study addresses the trade-off between capacity and latency in large language model (LLM) inference systems. While **High-Bandwidth Flash (HBF)** offers 16x higher capacity per stack compared to High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) with comparable bandwidth, replacing HBM with HBF alone risks performance degradation due to HBF's long tail latency, which starves GPU schedulers. To mitigate this, the team proposes a **Heterogeneous Memory Architecture (HMA)** that integrates HBM and HBF using a **prediction-based migration policy**, ensuring high-latency HBF is kept off the GPU's critical path. This approach balances capacity and performance for efficient LLM inference.  

**Paper Link:** https://doi.org/10.1109/LCA.2026.3723326 

[M3D 6T SRAM With BEOL Pass-Gates at 2nm (Georgia Tech, Synopsys)](https://semiengineering.com/m3d-6t-sram-with-beol-pass-gates-at-2nm-georgia-tech-synopsys/) 
Researchers from the Georgia Institute of Technology and Synopsys published a paper titled **“A Process-Aware Hybrid Si/IGO Monolithic-3D 6T SRAM with BEOL Pass-Gates for the 2nm Node”** (August 2026). The study introduces a **monolithic-3D (M3D) 6T SRAM** for the 2nm node, integrating **BEOL IGO pass-gates**, an **all-silicon nanosheet latch**, **buried power rails**, and **Ru interconnects**. Key results include a **25% reduction in cell footprint** compared to a high-performance silicon baseline and a **42.2% decrease in write delay** at the subarray level. The work highlights advancements in hybrid silicon/indium-gallium-oxide (IGO) integration for next-generation memory technologies. [DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2608.22741](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.22741) 

[Intrinsic Polarization Superjunctions Boost GaN-On-Silicon Power Devices (EPFL)](https://semiengineering.com/intrinsic-polarization-superjunctions-boost-gan-on-silicon-power-devices-epfl/) 
Researchers from École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL) published a paper in *Nature Electronics* (August 2026) titled *“Intrinsic Polarization Superjunctions in III-Nitride Heterostructures for Efficient Power Electronics.”* The study addresses challenges in III-nitride semiconductors, such as electric-field crowding and trapping effects, which limit their performance in power electronics. By introducing intrinsic polarization superjunctions in III-nitride heterostructures, the team aims to enhance breakdown voltage, improve dynamic performance, and boost reliability for cost-effective, high-efficiency power devices. The paper is open access. 

[Cycle-Level Simulator for Distributed GPUs For AI Workloads (Purdue)](https://semiengineering.com/cycle-level-simulator-for-distributed-gpus-for-ai-workloads-purdue/) 
Researchers from Purdue University published a paper titled “Architecting the Next Generation of Asynchronous, Distributed GPUs for the AI Era” in August 2026. The study introduces a cycle-level simulation framework designed for modern GPU architectures, including Ampere, Hopper, and Blackwell. The framework was validated against physical silicon, achieving a 99% Pearson correlation coefficient on H100 GPU results, demonstrating its accuracy. The paper is available on arXiv via DOI: [10.48550/arXiv.2608.22602](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.22602). 

[Molecular Dynamics Study Explains Aluminum Dopant Activation In 4H-SiC (TU Wien, Silvaco)](https://semiengineering.com/molecular-dynamics-study-explains-aluminum-dopant-activation-in-4h-sic-tu-wien-silvaco/) 
Researchers from TU Wien and Silvaco Europe conducted a molecular dynamics (MD) study to investigate how implantation temperature and dose affect dopant activation in Al-implanted 4H-SiC. The study, published in *Journal of Materials Chemistry C* (2026), focused on experimentally observed non-monotonic behavior of Al dopant activation with temperature. Using MD simulations at 500 K and 900 K with doses ranging from 1×10¹³ to 7.5×10¹⁴ cm⁻², the team analyzed defect formation, evolution, and dopant activation during early-stage annealing. The work applied an Al potential fitted to DFT-derived energy barriers to model atomic-scale mechanisms. The findings aim to clarify the atomistic origins of dopant activation in 4H-SiC under varying implantation conditions. [DOI: 10.1039/d6tc01299j] 

[Programmable Silicon Photonics Filter Enables Continuous Laser Wavelength Tuning (UiT, Stanford)](https://semiengineering.com/programmable-silicon-photonics-filter-enables-continuous-laser-wavelength-tuning-uit-stanford/) 
Researchers from UiT The Arctic University of Norway and Stanford University published a technical paper in *ACS Photonics* (August 2026) titled *"Intracavity Laser Wavelength Tuning by a Programmable Forward-Only Silicon Photonics Interferometer Mesh."* The study introduces a novel, mechanically robust, and tunable nonresonant silicon photonic filter designed to programmably adjust the wavelength of an erbium-doped fiber laser ring cavity. The filter combines forward-only interferometer meshes with a waveguide delay line array, enabling fully configurable and tunable spectral filtering. The work demonstrates the practical application of this silicon photonic technology for precise laser wavelength control, advancing integrated photonics for optical communication and sensing systems. [DOI: 10.1021/acsphotonics.6c00194]
