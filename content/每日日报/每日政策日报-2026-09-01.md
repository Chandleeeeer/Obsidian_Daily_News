### <img src="https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?sz=32&domain=semiengineering.com" width="32" height="32" /> Semiconductor Engineering
[Workload-Driven HBF Substrate For Capacity-Scalable LLM Inference (Huawei, ETH Zurich, HUST)](https://semiengineering.com/workload-driven-hbf-substrate-for-capacity-scalable-llm-inference-huawei-eth-zurich-hust/) 
**Summary of "FLINT: Efficiently Leveraging High Bandwidth Flash for Capacity-Scalable LLM Inference Acceleration"**  
Researchers from Huawei, ETH Zürich, and HUST propose **FLINT**, a novel framework to enhance large language model (LLM) inference efficiency by leveraging **High Bandwidth Flash (HBF)**, a 3D-stacked NAND flash technology. Traditional LLM inference systems face bottlenecks due to limited accelerator memory (HBM), restricting model size. FLINT addresses this by integrating HBF as a capacity tier alongside HBM, optimizing three key challenges:  

1. **Dynamic HBF Read Management**: A hardware burst-buffer controller dynamically coalesces and pipelines HBF reads to maximize bandwidth while utilizing NAND flash buffers.  
2. **Phantom-Plane Refresh**: A mechanism isolates NAND flash refresh operations (critical for reliability) from the inference path, reducing latency by offloading them to low-cost resource duplication.  
3. **Read-Only FTL (Flash Translation Layer)**: Replaces traditional write-supporting FTLs with a compact table mapping logical weight bursts to physical HBF locations, simplifying management for read-heavy LLM inference.  

FLINT aims to scale LLM inference capacity without sacrificing performance, enabling larger models on single-accelerator systems. The paper was published as a preprint in August 2026.  

**Paper**: [arXiv:2608.25062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25062)  
**Authors**: Oliveira, Geraldo F., Tavakkol, Arash, Zhu, Xiangyu, et al.

[Photonics Forces A Chiplet Rethink](https://semiengineering.com/photonics-forces-a-chiplet-rethink/) 
**Summary:**  
Optical chiplets are transforming chip design by moving from a placement-focused approach to a complex **multi-physics co-design problem**, where thermal, mechanical, electromagnetic, and optical interactions must be managed bidirectionally. As optical components approach silicon, they improve bandwidth and power efficiency but introduce challenges like heat management, alignment, and reliability. Chip architects must prioritize traffic patterns, system architecture, and interface contracts before integrating optics.  

Key advancements include **TSMC’s COUPE** technology, which vertically stacks photonic integrated circuits (PICs) and electronic integrated circuits (EICs) using hybrid bonding, reducing signal latency, power consumption, and server space. However, the closer optics are to logic, the more critical system-level co-design becomes, as thermal drift, mechanical stress, and electromagnetic coupling complicate interactions between photonic and electronic components.  

Industry players like TSMC, Intel, and STMicroelectronics are scaling photonics manufacturing, with co-packaged optics (CPO) gaining traction in AI data centers. Challenges persist in system-level integration, requiring holistic design approaches to balance performance, reliability, and thermal management. The shift from copper to optics is no longer about replacing a medium but redefining chiplet architecture as a unified, tightly coupled system.
