- **Switching Speed**: SSRs switch faster, improving response times, whereas mechanical relays have slower, mechanical switching.  
- **Power Loss**: SSRs exhibit lower power loss due to reduced resistance, while mechanical relays incur higher losses from contact resistance.  
- **EMI**: Mechanical relays generate electromagnetic interference (EMI) from arcing contacts, while SSRs produce minimal EMI.  
- **PCB Space**: SSRs like the MP9566 occupy less space on printed circuit boards (PCBs) compared to mechanical relays.  

The comparison highlights the MP9566 as a compact, efficient alternative for thermostat applications requiring reliability and low EMI.

[The imec Report: Advancing The CFET-Based Device Roadmap](https://semiengineering.com/advancing-the-cfet-based-device-roadmap-novel-integration-modules-and-standard-cell-configurations/) 
**Summary:**  
The semiconductor industry is transitioning from FinFETs to gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheet transistors for advanced nodes (3nm and beyond), with further scaling likely through complementary FET (CFET) architectures. CFETs enable smaller standard cells (e.g., 3T) by stacking p and nMOS transistors, extending CMOS scaling to at least the A3 node. Two CFET integration approaches are highlighted: **monolithic CFET (mCFET)** (single-process flow with complex alignment challenges) and **sequential CFET (sCFET)** (multi-step process with wafer flips and alignment issues, slower to adopt).  

Key advancements include:  
1. **Process modules** for CFETs, such as a backside contact module and gate-stack integration enabling multiple threshold voltages (Vt) in nanosheet and CFET devices.  
2. **Design-technology co-optimization (DTCO)** studies exploring scalable standard cell configurations, split-gate/common-gate architectures for logic and SRAM scaling, and improved back-end-of-line (BEOL) routability.  
3. **Imec’s roadmap** emphasizes CFET-specific innovations, with progress supported by the European NanoIC pilot line targeting beyond-2nm systems-on-chip (SoC).  

CFETs are positioned as a critical enabler for next-generation logic and memory scaling, driven by AI and heterogeneous computing demands. While mCFET offers faster industrial adoption, sCFET’s challenges highlight the need for alignment and process advancements. 

[Research Bits: Sept. 1](https://semiengineering.com/research-bits-sep-1/) 
**Summary of GaN Research Advances:**  

1. **High-Voltage GaN Transistors (iPSJ):** Researchers at EPFL developed an intrinsic polarization superjunction (iPSJ) GaN transistor that can withstand nearly 4 kV with minimal energy loss, surpassing previous records. The design uses layered GaN on silicon, leveraging natural polarization effects to balance charge sheets and eliminate the need for chemical doping. This addresses challenges in high-voltage power electronics, enabling use in EVs and industrial systems where reliability under extreme conditions is critical.  

2. **Low-Resistance p-GaN Contacts:** A team from Nagoya University and collaborators reduced p-type GaN contact resistance by applying an ultrathin magnesium layer (1–3 × 10⁻⁴ Ω cm²) via a simple, cost-effective annealing process. This method avoids surface damage and impurities, offering a faster alternative to traditional crystal growth techniques and promising applications in EV power transistors and LEDs.  

3. **Diamond-Embedded GaN for Thermal Management:** MIT and partner institutions embedded GaN transistors in diamond interposers to improve heat dissipation. This integration equalizes temperatures between GaN and silicon, enhancing reliability and performance in wireless power amplifiers without compromising efficiency. The approach combines the strengths of materials for next-gen 3D integrated systems.  

These innovations highlight advancements in GaN technology for high-voltage, low-loss, and thermally stable power electronics, with potential impacts on EVs, industrial systems, and wireless communication devices.
