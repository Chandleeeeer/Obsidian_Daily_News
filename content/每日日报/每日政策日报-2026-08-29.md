[Ferroelectric Tuning Reduces Optical-Interconnect Stalls In LLM Training (Georgia Tech)](https://semiengineering.com/ferroelectric-tuning-reduces-optical-interconnect-stalls-in-llm-training-georgia-tech/) 
Researchers from the Georgia Institute of Technology published a technical paper titled "Thermal Tuning Overhead in Wafer-Scale Optical Interconnects for LLM MoE Training: A Cross-Layer Analysis and Ferroelectric-Based Mitigation." The study examines wafer-scale optical interconnects used in large language model (LLM) training with a mixture-of-experts (MoE) approach. It identifies repeated tuning stalls during communication phases, which significantly impact performance. By eliminating these stalls, the paper reports speedups of 2.7x for Mixtral 8x7B. The researchers propose a ferroelectric-based mitigation strategy to address this issue. The full paper is available on arXiv as of August 2026. 

[Generative Design Optimizes Liquid-Cooling Channels For 2.5D And 3D Packages (UM-Dearborn)](https://semiengineering.com/generative-design-optimizes-liquid-cooling-channels-for-2-5d-and-3d-packages-um-dearborn/) 
Researchers from the University of Michigan-Dearborn have published a technical paper titled "Generative Design of Liquid-Cooling Channels for Thermal Management of 2.5D and 3D Integrated Advanced Packaging." The study presents a physics-guided generative design framework used to optimize liquid cooling channel topologies in a complex multi-chip package, which includes two high-power GPUs and one CPU. The framework was applied to a "2.7 kW multi-chip package" with the goal of improving thermal management.

The results indicate that using this method (referred to as G1016) reduced the maximum GPU temperature by 33.6%, the temperature spread by 52.5%, and the pressure drop by 72.8% compared to conventional cooling methods. The paper is available on arXiv under the title "Generative Design of Liquid-Cooling Channels for Thermal Management of 2.5D and 3D Integrated Advanced Packaging" (Acquah, Michael & Liu, Zheng, August 2026; doi:10.48550/arXiv.2608.22787). 

[Sapphire Surface Modification Enables Wafer-Scale 2D Semiconductor Growth (Peking U., CAS et al.)](https://semiengineering.com/sapphire-surface-modification-enables-wafer-scale-2d-semiconductor-growth-peking-u-cas-et-al/) 
Researchers from Peking University and the Chinese Academy of Sciences published a paper in *Nature Communications* titled "Homo-Metal-Element Mediated Surface Modification of Sapphire for Robust Epitaxy of Wafer-Scale Single-Crystalline 2D Semiconductors." The study introduces a method to modify the surface of commercially available C/M sapphire using homo-metal-elements (such as W, Mo, and Al) to achieve robust epitaxy of single-crystal 2-inch scale monolayers of WS2 or MoS2. This technique is significant for advancing the development of large-scale, high-quality 2D semiconductor materials. 

[Deep Learning Automates Parameter Extraction For 2D Transistors (Stanford, SLAC)](https://semiengineering.com/deep-learning-automates-parameter-extraction-for-2d-transistors-stanford-slac/) 
Researchers from Stanford University and SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory have published a technical paper titled "Deep Learning to Automate Parameter Extraction and Model Fitting of Two-Dimensional Transistors." The study presents a deep learning approach that automates the extraction of physical parameters (such as mobility, Schottky contact barrier height, and defect profiles) from electrical measurements of 2D transistors, facilitating automated parameter extraction and technology computer-aided design (TCAD) fitting. To support this process, the researchers implemented a data augmentation and pretraining approach using a secondary neural network to approximate a physics-based device simulator. The paper was released in August 2026 by the authors: Robert K. A. Bennett, Jan-Lucas Uslu, Harmon F. Gault, Asir Intisar Khan, Lauren Hoang, Tara Peña, Kathryn Neilson, Young Suh Song, Zhepeng Zhang, Andrew J. Mannix, and Eric Pop, under a Creative Commons license.
